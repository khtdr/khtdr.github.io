#+BEGIN_QUOTE
*The Well Rounded Climber* (tWRC) is a climbing log with a thesis: *track habits, not
grades*. A V4 at your gym isn't a V4 at mine, and it certainly isn't a V4 outside — so the
app refuses to build a leaderboard out of a number that doesn't travel. It measures the two
things that do: how your difficulty is *distributed*, and how varied your *style* is.

** Installing a sideloaded APK

Android blocks installs from outside the Play Store until you allow it, per-app:

1. Download the =.apk= above on the phone itself (or copy it over).
2. Tap it. Android will offer to let your browser or file manager install unknown apps —
   grant it, then come back and tap the file again.
3. Play Protect may warn you that it doesn't recognize the developer. It doesn't; I'm not in
   the Play Store. Install anyway if you're comfortable with that.

Once installed, updates to the app's JavaScript arrive over the air on launch — you only need
a new APK when the native side changes.

* Why not grades?

Grades are the whole vocabulary of climbing and they're almost useless as a training signal.
They're gym-relative, setter-relative, and style-relative, and chasing them produces a very
specific kind of stuck climber: one who only ever tries the thing they're already good at,
one grade below their limit, on the wall angle they like.

So tWRC never asks what a route was graded. It asks two questions instead.

* The Pyramid — are you distributing your effort?

Every climb gets self-classified into one of three *personal* buckets, defined against your
ability today rather than any gym's scale:

- *Base* — easy mileage. Volume, movement, warm-up.
- *Core* — hard but doable. Your working level.
- *Stretch* — at or past your limit. Projects.

A healthy season is pyramid-shaped: a lot of base, a solid core, a few stretch. The chart
shows you the shape you actually climbed.

It deliberately ships *no score, no percentage, and no verdict*. What it gives you instead is
a named gap, where you sat, and one small ask you can go verify:

#+BEGIN_QUOTE
Last session: 8 base, 13 core, 1 stretch. Your last 6 weeks run core-heavy.

Next session: pick two slab problems.
#+END_QUOTE

There's a design landmine buried here that took a rewrite to find. The bars are drawn from
*effort* (attempts plus a completion bonus), but the judgement about which way your training
*leans* is computed from plain *counts* — one climb, one climb. Score the lean on effort
instead and a single twelve-go project outweighs an eight-problem warm-up block, so every
session where you genuinely projected reads as "top-heavy." Sandbagging scored four times
better than trying hard, at exactly the climber this app exists for. Now a project is one
climb, because it *is* one climb.

* The Spiders — are you climbing everything?

Three radar charts, tagged in the moment on any climb:

- *Hold Type* — crimp, sloper, pinch, jug, pocket, volume, crack
- *Wall Angle* — slab, vertical, overhang, roof, arete, stem
- *Movement* — dyno, static, mantle, compression, lock-off

Tags aren't exclusive: one climb can be a crimpy overhanging lock-off, and it counts in all
three webs. Spokes are a raw tally — every tagged climb counts once, onsight or fifteen-
attempt failure alike, because the question a web answers is "have you touched this?" and a
climb answers that once.

The tally only becomes useful with a denominator, so the webs report in sentences:

#+BEGIN_QUOTE
You logged 4 slab problems in 90 days, out of 210 climbs.
#+END_QUOTE

"4 slab" reads as a preference. "4 out of 210" is a gap.

** The taxonomy is yours

Those eighteen dimensions are a starting point, not a rule. Your gym doesn't set cracks? A
crack spoke pinned at zero forever drags the web inward and reads as a gap nobody can close
— so turn it off. Add your own. Turning one back on restores its whole history; nothing is
deleted when it's hidden.

* Logging has to be fast or it doesn't happen

The whole app dies if logging is a chore between burns, so the form is aggressive about it:

- *Level picks the form.* Base asks nothing but "how many." Core asks the result and offers
  a one-tap "battled it." Stretch — the only place a count actually matters — asks for
  attempts on a keypad. One form serves three very different climbers, and it adapts
  *within* a single session, which a global "advanced mode" toggle never could.
- *Tags carry over* from your last climb within a session, so a session on the same wall is
  one tap per problem. They're never persisted overnight — last night's overhang has no
  business attaching to tomorrow's slab session.
- *There's an end-of-session pass* that offers up today's untagged climbs with a handful of
  recallable chips, for when you were too pumped to care at the time.
- *No modes, no onboarding questionnaire.* Nothing to configure before you can log a climb.

* Make it yours

Pick three colors — canvas, accent, and a chart seed — and the app derives its entire theme
from them. Every text color is checked against every background it lands on and corrected
until it clears WCAG AA contrast, including for deliberately awful picks, because the failure
mode of a bad color choice is an unreadable app whose only escape hatch is a settings screen
rendered in the theme you can't read.

* Your data stays on your phone

Everything lives in a SQLite database on the device. No account, no server, no sync, no
analytics, no ads. Nothing to sign up for and nothing leaves the phone.

* Built with

Expo / React Native and TypeScript on SDK 56, expo-router for navigation, expo-sqlite with
Drizzle ORM for typed on-device storage and migrations, Zod for schemas, and EAS for builds
and over-the-air updates. Charts are hand-rolled SVG.
