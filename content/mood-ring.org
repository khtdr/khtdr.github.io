*Mood Ring* is a small Android app for noticing how you've been feeling. Now and then it asks
a quick question, it has a short check-in at the end of the day, and it offers an occasional
questionnaire. Then it shows you how things change over time. I made it for a small circle of
friends and family, so it is not in the Play Store.

* Install

** 1. Let your browser install apps

Android only installs apps from the Play Store until you give permission, and you give it one
app at a time.

1. Open the download link above on your phone.
2. When it finishes, tap the downloaded file. Android will say your browser (or file manager)
   isn't allowed to install unknown apps. Tap *Settings*, turn on *Allow from this source*, then
   go back.
3. Tap *Install*.
4. Play Protect may warn you that it doesn't recognize the developer. That's expected, because
   the app isn't in the Play Store. Tap *More details*, then *Install anyway*, if you're
   comfortable with that.

You can turn *Allow from this source* back off afterward. It isn't needed for updates.

** 2. Let the reminders through

The first time you open Mood Ring, it asks to send notifications. Say yes, or it can't remind
you to check in.

Many phones also shut down background apps to save battery, which can make reminders show up
late or not at all. If your reminders don't arrive:

1. Open Android *Settings → Apps → Mood Ring → Battery*.
2. Choose *Unrestricted* (some phones call it *Don't optimize*).

Samsung, Xiaomi, OnePlus, Huawei, and some other brands add their own extra battery settings.
[[https://dontkillmyapp.com][dontkillmyapp.com]] has step-by-step instructions for each brand.

** Updates

Most updates arrive on their own. The app checks for one when it opens and asks before it
restarts to apply it. You only need to download a new APK when the version number on this page
changes. You can see which version you have in the app under *Settings → About Mood Ring*.

** Uninstalling

Uninstalling Mood Ring deletes everything you've entered in it, unless Android backup is on (see
below). If you want to keep your history, use *Export JSON* or *Export CSV* under *Settings → Your data* first and save the file
somewhere safe.

* What it does

- *Quick nudges.* A few times a day, at random times within hours you choose, it asks "how are
  you feeling right now?" Answering is one tap on a five-point scale from /Awful/ to /Great/,
  plus an optional energy rating.
- *An end-of-day check-in.* A mood for the day, an optional note, and any questions you've added
  yourself. For example, "Did you exercise?" (yes/no), "How well did you sleep?" (a 1–5 rating), or
  "How many coffees?" (a count).
- *Occasional questionnaires.* Short, widely used screening questionnaires, each on a schedule
  you pick, such as every two weeks:
  - GAD-7 (anxiety)
  - PHQ-9 (depression)
  - K10 (psychological distress)
  - PSS-10 (stress)
  - WHO-5 (wellbeing)
  - UCLA 3-item (loneliness)
  - Satisfaction With Life Scale
- *History.* A calendar colored by mood, and each day's entries in detail.

The reminders are deliberately vague, like "How are you feeling right now?" or "How was today?" They never show a mood, a
score, or the name of a questionnaire, because notifications can show up on your lock screen.

** It is not a diagnosis

Mood Ring is not a diagnostic tool, and it doesn't replace professional care. The questionnaires
are screening tools. If you're worried about how you've been feeling, a doctor or other health
professional can help. The app has an /If you need help now/ screen with crisis lines. It works
offline. In the US, you can call or text *988* at any time.

* Privacy

- *It stays on your phone.* Everything you enter is saved in a database on your phone. There is
  no account to create and no server to sync to.
- *Nobody else sees it.* There are no ads, no analytics, no crash reporting, and no third
  parties. The only thing the app ever sends over the internet is the check for app updates, and
  that check sends none of your data.
- *Your own Google backup.* Android backs up app data to your own Google account, so your
  history can come back when you move to a new phone. If you'd rather it didn't, turn it off in
  Android *Settings → Google → Backup*. That setting applies to the whole phone.
- *You can take it with you, or delete it.* *Settings* can export everything to a JSON or CSV
  file that you save wherever you want, or delete all of it from the phone.
